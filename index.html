The gap

Anonymous today

Fills with strangers. Anyone can write to anyone, which means most of what arrives is noise, and the volume turns people into content — the moment a sender sees a visible “12 confessions received,” they optimise for cruelty instead of honesty.

Freedom to speak. No reason to.

Named today

Solves the reason by solving the exposure. A handle is a consistent distinguishing mark, and in a campus of 30,000 with a dense graph, “you recognise that gradient” is all the deanonymisation anyone needs.

The reason. No freedom.

Students don’t need a place to speak. They need somewhere to say the thing and have it land with the right person.

The loop, in five moves

  1. 01

    Verify

    18+ and a .edu address. No K‑12, no exceptions — the age floor is a legal constraint, not a content setting.

  2. 02

    Build a circle

    People you already have a verified connection to. Suggested by mutual degree, never by looks. Every add opens a channel, and three channels are required before you can write.

  3. 03

    Write

    To one person or a 5–8 person Circle Drop. Draft-first, prompt seeds, and a pre-send tone pass. PII is a hard block; everything else is advisory.

  4. 04

    Deliver

    Cover traffic, 10–90 minute jitter, and cohort floors, so arrival time reveals nothing about send time. A 48‑hour dissolve if unopened, which removes the social pressure to check.

  5. 05

    Sign it

    Either side may reveal their name. Only voluntarily, only mutually, never silently, never to anyone else. Forever optional, both ways.

Six things this will never do

Not “not in v1.” Not “behind a flag.” Each one is a product decision with a reason, and each would need a founder-level reversal.

  • No cold DMs. A channel must exist before you can write. This one rule removes most abuse before moderation is involved.
  • No avatars. Not a silhouette, not initials, not a generated gradient. Anonymity is enforced by the absence of a distinguishing mark, so the absence has to be architectural.
  • No counts. No reply counts, no follower counts, no view counts. Counts turn people into metrics.
  • No public feed. The most-requested and most dangerous feature. A graph product with a feed is a broadcast product, and broadcast is the opposite of the promise.
  • No images or voice. In a small graph a photo is an identity. Revisiting this needs a new anonymity design, not an upload endpoint.
  • No paid visibility. A purchased impression is a lie about a relationship. No ads in the content surface, ever.

The moment

Sign it

The identity that “knows” you is never the person you’re talking to. So the payoff isn’t anonymity — it’s the moment someone chooses to put their name to what they said to you, and you find out it was the person you hoped.

  • Mutual. You are asked separately, and their answer is never shown to you.
  • Voluntary. Signing is never a condition of anything, and never expires as an option.
  • Never silent. No one can be revealed without their own action. There is no server-side unmasking path.

No one else on campus can see either of your names. Not even moderators.

How the anonymity actually works

A promise nobody can check is a slogan. These are the mechanisms, and the numbers are the floor, not the target.

Mechanism What it stops
cover trafficAn empty inbox is indistinguishable from a busy one, so volume carries no signal
10–90 min jitterArrival time cannot be back-solved into send time
cohort floor 250No campus can open a graph small enough to read
k ≥ 25No recipient is ever 1-of-N, so no single reader is an outlier
48h dissolveNothing waits forever to be opened, so nothing is leverage
no read receiptsYou cannot learn whether a specific person read you

The honest risk

In a small graph, a determined person may work out who sent something. That cannot be engineered away, and any product claiming otherwise is lying. What we can do is make the promise precisely true, make inference expensive and noisy, and say the limit plainly in the product itself. An app that overclaims here is worse than one that admits the limit.
